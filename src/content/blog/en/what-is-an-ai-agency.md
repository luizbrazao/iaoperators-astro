"I need AI in my company, but I don't know where to start or who to call." If that sounds like you, you're looking for exactly this: an AI agency. Let's walk through what it is, what it actually does — no fluff — and, above all, how not to get the choice wrong.

## Definition: what an AI agency is (and isn't)

An AI agency is a company that **designs, builds, and ships custom artificial intelligence systems** for other businesses. The key word is *builds*: it doesn't sell you a license to closed software and walk away. It sits down with your specific case, builds the solution, and leaves it running inside your operation.

And what it **isn't**: it's not a marketing agency using ChatGPT to write posts, nor a consultancy that hands you a slide deck of recommendations and disappears. An AI agency writes the code, connects your tools, and takes responsibility for the thing working on Monday morning.

## What an AI agency does: the services

In practice, almost everything an AI agency does fits into four buckets.

### Custom AI agents

An AI agent is like an employee that never sleeps: it takes a request, decides what to do, checks your systems, and acts — or flags a human when something goes off-script. Customer support that actually resolves, lead qualification, internal assistants that search your documentation. Not a form in disguise: an agent that reasons about your context.

### Process automation with AI

All that repetitive work someone does by hand today — copying data between apps, generating reports, moving information from one system to another — becomes a flow that runs on its own. This is where tools like n8n come in; if you want to go deeper, we have a [comparison of n8n, Zapier, and Make](/en/blog/n8n-vs-zapier-vs-make/).

### Chatbots and virtual assistants for businesses

Support over WhatsApp, web, or whichever channel you use, available 24/7 and in multiple languages. Done right, it's not a rigid button tree: it understands what the customer asks and resolves it or escalates. It's one of the most common entry points to AI because the return shows up fast.

### Integrating AI into your stack

AI doesn't live alone. The value appears when it connects to what you already use: your CRM, your WhatsApp, your database, your internal tools. A big part of an AI agency's job is exactly that glue — integrating, via API, systems that didn't talk to each other.

## How it differs from a marketing agency, a consultancy, or a software shop

It's the most common mix-up, so let's be clear:

- **Marketing agency:** uses AI *as a tool* for its own work (content, ads). It doesn't build systems for you.
- **Consultancy:** tells you *what* to do. Valuable for deciding, but often it doesn't touch the code or leave anything in production.
- **Software shop:** builds custom software, but not necessarily with AI or focused on automating your operation.
- **AI agency:** joins the two halves that matter — it decides *what* makes sense and *builds* it until it's running.

The difference fits in one sentence: it hands you **something that works, not a report**.

## When your company needs an AI agency

Not every company does, and we'd rather tell you. The signals that you do:

- Your team loses hours on repetitive, predictable tasks.
- You copy data from one app to another by hand.
- You answer the same questions over and over.
- You have data piling up that you don't use to decide anything.
- You know "this could be automated," but nobody internally has the time or the profile to do it.

If the bottleneck is **operational and repeatable**, it's a candidate. If it's strategic or creative, maybe what you need is something else.

## How to choose an AI agency: a 7-point checklist

This is where most people get it wrong, because the market is full of hype. The criteria that actually matter:

1. **Real technical experience, not a sales pitch.** Ask to see things they've built, not just pretty case studies on the website.
2. **Cases in production.** A demo is easy. The hard part — and the one that matters — is working with real data, every day.
3. **Observability.** How will you know if the system fails? A good agency sets up alerts and logs, not an agent that breaks in silence.
4. **Code ownership.** Is the system yours, or are you tied to the agency forever? It should be yours.
5. **Maintenance and handover.** They should train and document so your team doesn't depend on them indefinitely.
6. **Honesty about trade-offs.** If they promise magic with no downsides, be wary. Every system has limits, and a good agency tells you what they are.
7. **Focus on the problem, not the technology.** They should start by asking what hurts, not by selling you the latest model.

And a step before all of this: before hiring anyone, it helps to know *what* to automate first. Mapping your processes with a [technology roadmap](/en/blog/what-is-a-technology-roadmap/) is the way to do that.

## How much an AI agency costs

There's no single number, but there are three common models:

- **Fixed-scope project:** a defined scope at a fixed price. Good when the problem is clear.
- **Monthly retainer:** a fee for continuous evolution and support. Good when you want to build in phases.
- **Hourly:** a block of hours. Flexible, but it requires you to have a clear direction.

The mistake is looking only at the hourly rate. What matters is the **return**: how many hours of manual work the system removes, or how much revenue it unlocks. An agent that saves your team 40 hours a month pays for itself. And the reverse is just as true: cheap that doesn't work is the most expensive thing there is.

## Use cases by sector

The same technology solves very different problems depending on the sector: clinics and salons automating bookings and reminders over WhatsApp; firms extracting data from documents; ecommerce qualifying and serving leads around the clock; finance teams automating reports across systems. The common thread isn't the sector — it's having a repeatable process that eats people's time today.
