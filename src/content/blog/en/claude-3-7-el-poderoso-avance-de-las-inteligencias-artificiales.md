With each update in the universe of AIs, we become more impressed with the advances of this technology that never stops evolving. With the release of Claude 3.7, a new promise emerged on the horizon: a powerful and innovative model that seeks to balance advanced reasoning with fast and practical answers. This new version has been presented as a revolutionary hybrid model, capable of combining the speed of instant responses with an improved reasoning mode for more detailed and in-depth solutions.

If you are one of those who like to explore the functionalities of AIs, sit back and we will tell you everything you need to know, from the performance analysis mode to the prompt battles. Get ready to enter the ring of innovation!

## The Release of Claude 3.7: What Changed?

From the beginning, the Claude 3.7 defined its territory: a revolutionary hybrid model that combines the speed of instant responses with an improved reasoning mode for more detailed and in-depth solutions. This new version not only overcomes the limitations of 3.5, but also opens up new possibilities in code development and frontend interface design, thanks to the launch of **Claude Code**, a tool dedicated to simplifying the lives of developers.

Furthermore, Claude 3.7 brought a significant improvement in performance analysis and token limit. With support for up to **200,000 tokens** in the standard model and 128,000 in reasoning mode, it is clear that this update is aiming high, especially for those who need robust and sophisticated answers.

## Comparison with Claude 3.5 and Grok

A direct comparison was made between Claude 3.7 and its previous version, 3.5. The results? Claude 3.7 came out ahead in virtually all metrics related to code development. Both in aesthetics and functionality, it showed substantial improvements. However, when comparing with the Grok, a difficulty in obtaining sufficient data was revealed, leading to a more qualitative than quantitative analysis.

### Reasoning vs. Immediate Responses

An interesting point was the battle of reasoning between the models. When tested in reasoning mode, the Claude 3.7 lost some points to the Grok on more complex questions, such as questions related to philosophy or underrated human abilities. This is not necessarily a flaw, but rather an area where the Claude 3.7 still has room to grow. In execution tasks, such as code generation or structural analysis, it showed excellent performance.

## Practical Tests: When AI Shows What It Can Do

There is nothing better than getting to work, or in this case, letting the AI ​​show what it knows how to do. A test was carried out to create a classic "Snake" game in two environments: Claude 3.5 and 3.7. As lines of code were written, it became clear how the latest model delivered not only a functional solution, but also a more elegant and modern approach.

While 3.5 showed visual and functional inconsistencies, Claude 3.7 managed to produce much cleaner code, with better fluidity and ease of implementation. This demonstrates the strength of the model in tasks that require both attention to detail and creativity in execution.

### Writing Style Also in Play

Another fascinating test was writing style analysis, essential for content and creative professionals. Here, the Claude 3.7 shined, managing to extract nuances and specific stylistic features with greater depth and precision compared to the 3.5. This shows how the model is more attuned to the needs of content personalization and storytelling.

## Conclusion of the Fights: Who Came Ahead?

After several rounds of hands-on testing, a quick summary: Claude 3.7 definitely brought notable improvements in areas such as code development, writing style extraction, and visual analysis. Despite this, when the topic was deep reasoning, the Grok still had a slight advantage in more complex situations, proving that the world of AIs is far from absolute hegemony — and that's great! Competition means more innovation for us, the end users.
