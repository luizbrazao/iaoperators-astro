The future is here, and it comes from technology giants like Microsoft, Google and Nvidia. Can you imagine a quantum chip that uses materials that break the known rules of physics? Or an AI model that collaborates with scientists to find medical treatments? Well hold on, because this is not science fiction, it is real science and it is happening right now. Today we are going to dive into four impactful innovations that are marking a before and after in technology and science. Make yourself comfortable, this promises.

On today's menu we have: a new quantum processor that seems to come out of an episode of Black Mirror, AI that becomes a video game designer, artificial intelligences that act as collaborating scientists and models that revolutionize biomedicine. Let's start!

## Microsoft's "Major 1" quantum processor: Real science or technological hype

Microsoft just brought out the big guns with "Major 1," a chip they claim to be the world's first quantum processor based on topological cubes. Topological cubes? That's right, and apparently they are neither solid, liquid nor gaseous. Basically, we are talking about a material with such advanced properties that it seems like a joke, but it is not.

The promise of Mayor 1 is monumental: scaling up to a million cubes on a single chip. That, in practical terms, could revolutionize areas such as cryptography, materials simulation and finance. But beware, some experts are not entirely convinced. In forums such as Hacker News, they highlight that this technology could still be in its infancy, and that competitors such as Google or IBM are more focused on superconducting circuits (a more proven technology for now). Is it all hype or are we facing unprecedented progress? Time will tell.

## "Muse", Microsoft's bet for AI in video games

Imagine that an AI not only creates the story of a video game, but also designs game mechanics respecting its physical laws. Well, don't imagine it, because Microsoft has made it a reality with "Muse", its generative AI model designed for video games. And what a triumphant entry!

"Muse" has already shown muscle using the well-known video game Bleeding Edge. The interesting thing is that, through the "VAM World and Human Action" model, this AI understands the rules of the game and generates objects or components respecting those rules. What once took entire design and development teams can now be sketched by an AI model in a matter of hours.

In addition, Microsoft has opted for transparency: the model weights, from 200 million to 1.6 billion parameters, are available to the community. This opens up a world of possibilities, not only for large developers, but also for independent creators. Is a new era of creativity coming to us in video games? I would say yes.

### What does this mean for players?

In short: more original games, designed faster and with AI capable of collaborating with developers. He feels like a Super Saiyan, but in creative development.

## Google and its "AI co-scientist": the virtual Einstein

Let's leave the video games for a moment and put on our scientist hats. Google has developed a really impressive system called "AI co-scientist", a set of agents based on its Gemini 2.0 model. Your mission? Act as a virtual colleague for scientists, generating and validating hypotheses as if you were just another researcher in the room.

The versatility of the system is amazing. A practical example that has already been given: this digital Einstein has helped propose drug reuses to treat acute myeloid leukemia, confirmed by real experiments. This breakthrough not only saves time, but opens doors to discoveries that could have taken years to happen.

### How does the "AI co-scientist" work?

Gemini 2.0 coordinates a group of agents that perform specific tasks: idea generation, hypothesis evaluation, classification and concept review. Basically, it's like having a team of scientists working tirelessly 24 hours a day, but without ordering coffee.

## Nvidia revolutionizes biomedicine with "Evo2"

If you thought Nvidia only made graphics cards for gamers, think again. Their newest creation, “Evo2,” is designed to address some of the most complex challenges in biomedicine. It is an AI model specifically designed for genomic mapping and drug discovery.

They have not done this project alone. Nvidia has teamed up with the Ark Institute and Stanford University, and the result is impressive. "Evo2" can accelerate genetic sequencing, identify new drug molecules and optimize clinical trials, shortening years of research to months or even weeks.
